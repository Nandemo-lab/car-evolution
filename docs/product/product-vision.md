# Product Vision

Status: **Draft**
Scope: what Car Evolution provides as a product

This document is not an Architecture Document. It does not define why the experience is built the way it is (that belongs to [Laws of Experience](../architecture/laws-of-experience.md)), how the vehicle is produced (Production Pipeline), or what the vehicle is (Definitive Master Policy). It defines, in one page, what Car Evolution is as a product: who it is for, what it delivers, and how we know it worked.

## Mission

Car Evolution lets someone experience, through a single familiar car, how much time has quietly reshaped something they thought they already knew. We do not sell vehicle information, specifications, or comparisons. We deliver a shift in perception — the moment a viewer looks at an ordinary car differently than they did five minutes earlier.

## Audience

The person this is built for is not a car enthusiast chasing specifications. It is someone with an ordinary, often unremarkable relationship to the vehicle in question — a family car, a car they see every day, a car they never thought to look at closely. Car Evolution is for people who don't know they're interested in this yet.

## Core Experience

A single vehicle occupies the entire screen. Nothing else competes for attention. As the viewer moves through time — by scrolling, by touch — the car's shape quietly changes: proportions shift, lines soften or sharpen, the vehicle becomes an earlier version of itself. The first touch is met with headlights igniting. Only after each shape has been seen does its year appear. There is no tour, no gallery, no comparison table — one vehicle, one continuous motion through time.

## Experience Principles

- **One vehicle at a time.** The experience never asks the viewer to compare multiple cars at once; depth on one vehicle matters more than breadth across many.
- **Shape before name.** The viewer is always shown before they are told — this governs every product surface, not only the core interaction.
- **Silence is part of the product.** Moments with no UI, no label, and no explanation are a deliberate part of what we ship, not gaps to be filled later.
- **Accuracy is the product's credibility.** Because the experience asks viewers to trust their own perception, the underlying vehicle must be trustworthy — inaccurate geometry breaks the product's core promise, not just its polish.
- **Mobile-first, thumb-first.** The primary and default way this product is experienced is a single hand, on a phone, mid-scroll.

## Success Criteria

Car Evolution succeeds when a viewer, having finished the experience, looks at the same vehicle in real life and notices something they hadn't before. Signals we look for:

- The viewer moves through the full range of time rather than stopping partway.
- The viewer can describe what changed in the vehicle's shape without having been told the years or generations.
- The experience is recalled and mentioned again after the session ends, not only while it is open.
- The Final Question from Laws of Experience — *did time move?* — can be answered yes by someone who has never read that document.

Information density, specification accuracy in copy, and interaction smoothness are necessary conditions, but none of them are success on their own. Success is measured by a change in how someone sees a car they already knew.
