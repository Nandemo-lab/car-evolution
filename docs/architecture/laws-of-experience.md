# Laws of Experience

Status: **Approved**
Scope: why Car Evolution is built the way it is

## Purpose

This document defines why Car Evolution creates the experience it creates.

It does not define what the vehicle is, how it is produced, or how it is rendered. Vehicle identity and geometry belong to [Definitive Master Policy](definitive-master-policy.md). The production process belongs to [Production Pipeline](production-pipeline.md). This document precedes both — it is the reason those documents exist, not a restatement of what they contain.

Any implementation detail, modeling technique, or rendering choice is out of scope here. If a decision can be made by reading this document alone, it is a decision about *why*, not *how*.

## Foundation

**Trust the Viewer.**

The experience exists because we trust the viewer. The viewer does not need to be told what they are looking at. They can recognize change, notice absence, and draw their own conclusion. Every law below exists to protect that trust — nothing here is designed to instruct the viewer, only to let them see.

## Law I: Discovery Before Explanation

The vehicle changes first. Information appears later.

Perception must come before knowledge. The viewer's own eye is the first author of meaning — if a label or a year arrives before the shape has already spoken, the experience has explained instead of revealed, and the viewer's discovery has been replaced by an announcement.

This law governs the *order* of experience, not the *making* of any asset. It does not describe how a shape is produced — only that, whatever produces it, the shape must be permitted to arrive first.

## Law II: Do Not Fear Silence

Moments without explanation are intentional.

A gap with no text, no label, no motion is not an unfinished moment — it is the space in which the viewer looks. Filling it with UI, narration, or ornament closes the space before the viewer has used it. Silence is not the absence of design; it is a designed absence.

## Final Question: Did time move?

Every decision in this project is measured against one question: did time move?

Not: was information shown. Not: was the vehicle accurate. Not: was the interaction smooth. Those questions matter, but they are answered elsewhere — in the Definitive Master Policy, in the Production Pipeline, in engineering review. This document asks only whether the viewer felt time pass through the shape of a single car.

If the answer is yes, the experience has succeeded regardless of what else is true. If the answer is no, no amount of accuracy, polish, or information density can substitute for it.
